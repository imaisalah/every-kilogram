# The 30-second demo video

The official limit is **30 seconds** of video **with English subtitles**. A longer video is not eligible for Global Judging. The video follows the official "30 seconds of glory" structure:

- 0:00 who you are
- 0:02 the challenge and why it matters
- 0:05 the title and tagline
- 0:10 how it works, including how NASA data made it possible
- 0:20 the future (here folded into the closing card, after the results)

The game has a demo mode that plays this cut by itself with the subtitles burned in. You start OBS, press a key, and stop OBS after the closing card. Everything on screen is the real game running: the demo opens a solo session, sends the same actions the buttons send, and steers your astronaut on the surface. Nothing in the video is generated by SI.

## Quick start

1. Run the game: double-click `play.bat`, or `node tools/serve.mjs 8080`.
2. Open this address in Chrome or Edge, with your own team name, members and link:

   ```
   http://localhost:8080/?team=Kilogram%20Crew&members=Ana%20Silva,%20Omar%20Hassan&link=every-kilogram.example#demo-30
   ```

3. Wait for the "Showcase ready" card. Play the cut once all the way through first, so the browser has the 3D scenes, maps and characters cached; then reload the address.
4. Start recording in OBS, then click anywhere on the page or press a key (not Esc or Tab). That click also turns on the sound.
5. The closing card appears at 0:26.6 and stays up until you click again. Stop recording after it has been up for a few seconds.
6. Trim the recording so it starts on the first frame of the "Who we are" card and lasts 30.0 s. If it comes out a frame long, cut that frame; 30.0 s is a hard limit.

Esc stops the demo at any time and returns to the title screen. The demo never touches saved data: while it runs, everything the game writes to browser storage goes to a temporary in-memory layer that is thrown away at the end.

### The demo addresses

| Address | What it plays |
|---|---|
| `#demo-30` | The 30-second cut, with title cards and English subtitles. Use this for the submission. |
| `#demo` | The full showcase, about 1 min 57 s: the same game at a calmer pace, with more steps and longer subtitles. For a longer video (social media, the project page) or for slide stills. |
| `#demo-raw` | The full showcase with no subtitles or cards, as B-roll for your own edit. |

Query options (put them before the `#`):

- `team=`: the team name.
- `members=`: optional; leave it out if any member is under 18 (see the rules below).
- `link=`: the address of the playable game, shown on the closing card.
- `autostart=1`: starts without the click. Sound may then stay off.
- `view=tpp`: the surface over the astronaut's shoulder instead of the top-down crew view (the demo's autopilot steers better from above, so keep the default for the submission).

From code, `SMD.demo.run({ cut: '30', team, members, link })` starts the demo and returns a promise. It resolves with `{ ms, wallMs }`: the demo's own clock and the real time it took.

### Timing

The 30-second cut runs on the wall clock: it always ends at 30.0 s, and if the computer stalls while a 3D scene is being built (the capsule, the flight, the surface), that shot simply loses those frames. The full showcase does the opposite: each frame counts as at most 0.1 s, so every shot keeps its full time on a slow computer and the whole video gets longer. Record on a computer with a real graphics card, plugged in, with other tabs closed.

## What the demo plays

Mars ice mapper on Cadet, seed 42: the planner Salma Fawzy, a low polar orbit, 14 m² of panels, a radar sounder and a telescopic camera beside the starting neutron spectrometer, a big dish and loud radio, the "Some samples, spare air" landing kit and the cheapest rocket that lifts it all; the requirement "Find the ice". This mission was checked with the simulation: it survives the flight even if every problem is missed, and the crew lands, scans, throws samples overboard at the door and lifts off. The flight's 70 simulated seconds are fitted into 26 s (a timing option of the room), and the cabin talk and the flight are cut short. On the surface the demo skips ahead twice by stepping the surface simulation: to the first scan, then to the decision at the lander door.

## Recording guide (OBS Studio)

- **Window:** 1920×1080. In Chrome, press F11 for full screen, or use a 1920×1080 window and crop the browser chrome in OBS. Zoom 100%.
- **OBS:** Display Capture or Window Capture, 1920×1080, 60 fps, NVENC or x264 at 12-20 Mbps.
- **Audio:** the game's own sounds are fine (countdown beeps, engine, chimes). A voice-over is optional; if you record one, read the subtitle lines, which are written to fit the time. Use only music you have a licence for, and list it under References.
- **The pointer:** the gold ring shows where a player would tap. The real mouse cursor is hidden while the demo runs.
- **Check before you upload:**
  - The video is 30.0 seconds or shorter.
  - The subtitles are readable on a phone.
  - No NASA logo or insignia appears. The game shows none, so do not add any in the edit.
  - No face, name or voice of anyone under 18 appears.
  - No commercial product is promoted.
- **Upload:** YouTube, Public or Unlisted (not Private). Add `demo-30.srt` as English captions. Open the link in a private window to check that it plays without a login, then paste it into "Link to Project Demo".

## Rules this video follows (2025 guide; recheck the 2026 guide)

- Up to 30 seconds, with English subtitles.
- Hosted outside the Space Apps site, and opens with "no logins, permissions, or registrations".
- No NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
- No SI-generated images or video. If any were added, they would need a visible watermark.
- No names, voices or likenesses of anyone under 18. Leave `members=` out if that applies.
