# Teaching with Every Kilogram: a 45-minute lesson

For ages 13 and up. One computer per group of one to four students. No account, no install (see `README.md`). The game runs in English and Arabic.

**Learning goals.** After the lesson a student can explain (1) why a heavier part can force a bigger rocket, (2) why batteries and a recorder exist, (3) why a spacecraft cannot send home everything it measures, and (4) NASA's four mission segments: launch, space, ground and user.

## Plan

| Minutes | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Warm-up question on paper (the "before" check below) | Keep the papers; they are the "before" half of a before/after comparison. |
| 5-10 | Demo: open `/#demo-30` on the projector | Thirty seconds of the whole game. Ask: what do you think is hard about building a spacecraft? |
| 10-30 | Groups play one mission on Cadet | Suggest Nile from orbit (Earth orbit, about ten minutes) or Moon. Roles: planner, engineer, scientist, communications. Talking is part of the game: in flight the alarm and the fix are on different screens. |
| 30-38 | Debrief on the screen: "Why it happened", the score, **Mission review** | Ask each group to name one domino and which of the four segments it belongs to. |
| 38-43 | Fly again with one change, or the **Check what you learned** quiz | Compare the two scores. |
| 43-45 | Same warm-up questions on paper again (the "after" check) | Compare with the "before" papers. |

## Before/after check (paper)

Use the same three questions before and after. Answers are in the quiz screen's explanations.

1. Why can one heavier instrument force you onto a bigger rocket?
2. A spacecraft measured more data than it could send home. Name two things that limit how much it can send.
3. Name the four segments of a space mission.

## Using the learning log

After each flight, the game keeps one row in this browser: when, mission, difficulty, attempt number, score, CML-inspired game progress (1 to 4), and how many quiz questions were right. It holds no names. On the review screen, **Download learning log (CSV)** saves it as a file you can open in a spreadsheet. Because a group usually flies the same mission several times, attempt 1 against later attempts shows whether the design improved. It is not a formal test or a NASA CML assessment.

## Vocabulary used in the game

- **Segment:** one of the four parts of a mission system: launch, space, ground, user.
- **CML-inspired game progress:** a simplified learning ladder from a mission idea to a complete game design. NASA/JPL's CML framework is a source of inspiration; the game's levels do not assess NASA CML criteria.
- **Trade study:** comparing different designs to pick the best fit.
- **ConOps:** the plan of what happens in a mission and when.
- **Eclipse:** the part of an orbit spent in a planet's shadow.

## Honest limits

Numbers are simplified for play (see `docs/TEAM-BALANCE.md`). The game has not yet been trialled in a classroom; the learning log is there so a teacher can see for themselves.
